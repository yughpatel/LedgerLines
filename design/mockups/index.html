LedgerLines design directions

Four looks for the same two-page app. D combines Bahi and Lamp. Each mockup is clickable: change the month, filter and search transactions, add or edit one, delete one, sign in. Every screen works at 390px and 1366px, in light and dark.

Notes on your decisions

  1. Two pages with tabs and a bottom bar: agree, with one change. Add is the most common action, so it gets its own spot: a raised button in the middle of the phone bar and a button in the desktop header. Log out and theme move into an account menu. Lamp uses a sidebar on wide screens, which has room for Splits, Categories and Settings without crowding.
  2. One month picker: agree, but it needs backend work. GET /transactions/summary takes no year or month today; it always returns the current IST month. The category endpoints are all-time. So the picker needs year and month on the summary and on both category endpoints. Until then, the frontend could work these numbers out from GET /transactions, which returns every row, but that stops scaling once a user has a few thousand rows.
  3. Rows grouped by day: agree, and use them on desktop too. One component instead of a table plus a list. On desktop the row just shows more columns (category, time, edit and delete buttons). On phones, tap a row to edit; Delete lives inside the edit form.
  4. Search, filters and paging work client-side for now. The list endpoint has no filters or paging, so the mockup loads the month and filters in the browser, 20 rows at a time. Fine for one person's data. Server-side filters can come later without changing the design.
  5. Refunds show as +₹ with a Refund tag and a return-arrow icon, never in the income colour, and the type form says what a refund does. Expenses are not painted red in Bahi and Neem; normal spending isn't an error.
  6. Motion follows what you do, and only then. Tab underlines, switches and filter chips slide to the new choice. Totals count to the new month's figures, chart bars reshape, category bars slide to new widths and trend lines draw in. The add form rises from the bottom on phones and can be swiped down to close. A deleted row folds away; a new one flashes once. Typing in search doesn't animate anything. With "reduce motion" on, all of it switches off.
  7. The trend chart only shows a negative zone when a bucket really is negative. October 16 is one: the headphones refund was bigger than that day's spending. Every chart also has a hidden data table for screen readers, and arrow keys move through the days.
